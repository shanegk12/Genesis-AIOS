# Horstemeyer 2021 — Creationeering Reference

**Source:** Horstemeyer, M.F. (2021). *Creationeering: A Faith-Based Engineering Design Framework.* [Full citation to be confirmed by Shane]

## The Creationeering™ Phases (in order)

1. **Design** — Define the problem, objectives, constraints, and variables (OCV). Inspired by God as the original Designer.
2. **Analysis / Synthesis** — Model the system mathematically and structurally. Understand forces, stresses, energy flows.
3. **Procurement** — Identify and acquire materials, tools, and resources. Stewardship of creation.
4. **Logistics** — Plan the workflow: sequencing, scheduling, risk, supply chain.
5. **Assembly** — Build, integrate, and connect components into a functioning system.
6. **Performance** — Test and validate against objectives. Measure against the OCV.
7. **Sustainability** — Design for long life, efficiency, and minimal environmental impact.
8. **Death & Recycling** — End-of-life planning: disassembly, material recovery, circular economy.

## Core Frameworks

### OCV Method (Objective, Constraint, Variable)
- **Objective:** What the design must achieve (the goal).
- **Constraint:** What limits the design (fixed boundaries — budget, material, weight, time).
- **Variable:** What can be changed to optimize toward the objective within the constraints.
- Apply one OCV per lesson to a concrete design problem.

### Multiscale Modeling
- Connect macro-level observations (what you can see) to atomic-level causes (why it happens at the molecular/material level).
- Every engineering phenomenon has a root cause at smaller scales.
- Example: A bridge bending (macro) → metal grain boundaries deforming (micro) → atomic bonds stretching (atomic).

### Optimization
- Finding the best trade-off between competing variables.
- Use "optimization" not "Pareto Frontier" in student-facing content.
- Always frame as: given these constraints, which variable setting best achieves the objective?

## Faith Integration

- God as the original Engineer and Creator — engineering reflects His design principles.
- Stewardship of creation — engineers are responsible managers of materials, energy, and environment.
- Biblical anchors: cite specific book, chapter, and verse. Connect theological idea directly to the engineering principle.
- One biblical stewardship objective per lesson (in Learning Objectives section).
- One Biblical Reflection journal prompt per lesson (in Engineering Journal Task section).
