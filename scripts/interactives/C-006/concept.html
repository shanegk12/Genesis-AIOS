Intro to Systems Thinking

Concept Overview

What Makes Things Work? Systems All Around Us!

Have you ever wondered how a bicycle moves, or how your body knows to breathe? It's all thanks to systems! A system is like a team of parts working together to achieve a goal. From your school to a simple toaster, everything around you is a system with a purpose.

Inside the Machine: Subsystems and Processes

Just like a big team has smaller groups, many systems have subsystems – smaller systems working within the larger one. For example, a bicycle has a braking subsystem and a pedaling subsystem. These subsystems follow a series of steps, called a process, to turn inputs into useful outputs.

Fueling the System: Inputs and Outputs

Every system needs something to get started and something to show for its work. Inputs are what go into a system, like the energy from your legs pedaling a bike or the sunlight a plant uses. Outputs are what come out, such as the bike moving forward or the plant growing food. Understanding inputs and outputs helps us see how systems function.

God's Design: Order in Creation and Engineering

Just as engineers design systems with purpose, God designed our world with incredible order and interconnected systems. From the intricate systems in our bodies to the cycles of nature, we can see His thoughtful design. Learning about systems helps us appreciate the complexity of creation and how God sustains everything with purpose.

Key Takeaway

Systems thinking helps us understand how parts work together to achieve a goal. By recognizing systems, subsystems, inputs, outputs, and processes, we can better understand the world around us and even see reflections of God's amazing design in creation.
