Novelty and Innovation in Engineering

Concept Overview

The Spark of Creation: Invention vs. Innovation

Imagine inventing a brand new game – that's an invention! Now, imagine someone makes that game even more fun to play, maybe by adding new rules or better pieces. That's innovation! We'll explore how inventing is about creating something totally new, while innovating is about making something existing much better and more useful.

From 'Aha!' to 'Awesome!': Types of Innovation

Not all improvements are the same. Sometimes, an innovation makes a small, steady change, like making a phone battery last a little longer (incremental improvement). Other times, an innovation completely changes how we do things, like when smartphones replaced flip phones (radical innovation). We'll also look at 'breakthroughs' – those big leaps that change everything!

Why Being New Matters: The Power of Novelty

Why can't you just copy someone else's great idea? Because engineers need their ideas to be 'novel' – truly new and different! We'll learn how engineers research 'prior art' (what's already out there) to make sure their inventions are original and can even be protected by patents. Novelty isn't just cool; it's super important for engineers!

Building on What's Been Built: Prior Art and Originality

Think of it like building with LEGOs. You wouldn't try to invent a new LEGO brick if one already exists, right? Engineers study 'prior art' to see what solutions already exist. This helps them find new problems to solve or ways to make existing solutions even better, ensuring their work is truly 'original' and makes a real difference.

Key Takeaway

In engineering, inventing is about creating something entirely new, like the first lightbulb. Innovating is about making something existing better and more useful, like an energy-saving LED bulb. Both are crucial for progress, and understanding the difference helps us build a better future!
