Standardization vs. Custom Fabrication

Concept Overview

What's the Difference?

Imagine you need a wheel for your project. A standardized wheel is like a LEGO wheel – it's already made, fits many things, and you can buy it easily. A custom-fabricated wheel is like designing and building a wheel from scratch just for your unique robot, making it exactly how you want it.

When to Go Standard

Choosing standardized parts, like those LEGO wheels, usually saves you time and money. They are often cheaper because many are made, and you can get them quickly. They also tend to be reliable because they've been tested and used by lots of people.

When to Go Custom

Sometimes, a standard part just won't do the job perfectly. If your project needs something super specific, like a gear with a very unusual number of teeth or a part that fits into a really tight space, custom fabrication is the way to go. It gives you complete control over the design and materials.

Thinking Like a Creationeer

Making the choice between standard and custom is a big part of being a smart Creationeer. You'll use the OCV method (Objectives, Constraints, Variables) to think through what's most important for your project – is it speed, cost, or a perfect fit? This helps you make the best decision, just like a good steward of resources.

Key Takeaway

Choosing between standardized and custom-fabricated parts is a fundamental decision in engineering. Standard parts save time and money, while custom parts offer unique solutions. A wise Creationeer knows when to use each, making choices that honor God through excellent design and careful use of resources.
