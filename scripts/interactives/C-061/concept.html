What is Performance?

Concept Overview

Performance: More Than Just 'Working'

Performance isn't just about whether something turns on; it's about how well it does what it's supposed to do. We measure performance by looking at things like how much output it creates, how efficient it is, and how reliable it is over time. This helps us understand if our designs are truly fulfilling their purpose.

Energy: The Engine of Performance

Every system needs energy to perform, and understanding how energy moves and changes is key. We'll explore how energy transfers from one place to another and transforms from one type to another, like chemical energy becoming motion. This helps us see where energy might be wasted and how we can use it more wisely.

Zooming In: Macro to Atomic Performance

Sometimes, to understand why something isn't working well on a big scale, we need to look at the tiny parts. Multiscale Modeling lets us connect what we see happening with our eyes (macro-level) to the invisible causes at the atomic level. This helps us find the root of problems and design better solutions.

Optimizing for God's Glory: The OCV Method

To make our systems the best they can be, we use the OCV method: Objectives, Constraints, and Variables. We identify our goal (Objective), what limits us (Constraints), and what we can change (Variables). This helps us optimize our designs, always striving for efficiency and effectiveness as good stewards of God's creation.

Key Takeaway

Performance is about observing, measuring, and improving how our engineered systems work, with energy at its core. By connecting big-picture results to tiny atomic causes and using tools like OCV, we can optimize our designs to be more efficient and effective, reflecting wise stewardship of God's resources.
