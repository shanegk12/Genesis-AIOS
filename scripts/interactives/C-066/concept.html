Functional Requirements and Specifications

Concept Overview

What Does It Need to Do? – Functional Requirements

Imagine you're building a robot to water plants. A 'functional requirement' is what that robot *must do*. It needs to move to the plant, detect if the soil is dry, and then pour water. These are the actions or behaviors essential for the robot to achieve its main purpose.

How Well Does It Need to Do It? – Specifications

Now, it's not enough for the robot to just 'pour water.' How much water? How quickly? How close to the plant? 'Specifications' are the exact, measurable details. For example, 'pour 100ml of water,' or 'detect dry soil within 5 seconds.' These details make sure our design works precisely as intended and helps us measure its success.

Designing with Purpose: The OCV Method

The OCV method (Objective, Constraint, Variable) helps us create super clear specifications. Your 'Objective' is your main goal, like 'create a plant-watering robot.' 'Constraints' are your limits, such as 'must be under $50' or 'fit on a windowsill.' 'Variables' are the parts you can change in your design to meet your objective within those limits, like the type of pump or the size of the water tank.

God's Design: Precision in Creation

Think about the incredible detail in God's creation, from the tiny parts of a cell to the vastness of the universe. Everything has a purpose and works with amazing precision. Just like God designed the world with perfect functional requirements and specifications, we strive for that same excellence and stewardship in our engineering projects, honoring His example.

Key Takeaway

Functional requirements tell us *what* our engineered system needs to do, and specifications tell us *how well* it needs to do it, with clear, measurable details. Using methods like OCV helps us design with purpose and precision, ensuring our creations are effective and honor the resources God has given us.
