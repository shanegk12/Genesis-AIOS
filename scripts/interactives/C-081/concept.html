The Ethics of End-of-Life (EOL) Responsibility

Concept Overview

From Skateboard to Stewardship: What is 'End-of-Life'?

Just like your favorite skateboard eventually wears out, every product reaches its 'end of life.' This isn't just about throwing things away; it's about our responsibility as 'Junior Creationeers' to think about what happens next. We'll learn how engineers plan for this stage, keeping God's creation in mind.

God's Design, Our Responsibility: The 'Death & Recycling' Phase

God designed our world with cycles of renewal, and we, as His image-bearers, are called to be good stewards. The 'Death & Recycling' phase in Creationeering™ is where we decide if a product's materials can be reused or turned into something new. This lesson explores how our Christian worldview guides us in making these important choices.

Seeing the Unseen: Multiscale Modeling and OCV

Have you ever wondered why a material breaks down? We'll use 'Multiscale Modeling' to connect what we see (like a cracked skateboard deck) to what's happening at a tiny, atomic level. We'll also use the OCV method (Objectives, Constraints, Variables) to help us design the best end-of-life plan for products.

Key Takeaway

Responsible 'Death & Recycling' isn't just an engineering problem; it's a biblical mandate. By understanding how products break down and planning for their end-of-life, we honor God's creation and act as wise stewards of the resources He has given us.
