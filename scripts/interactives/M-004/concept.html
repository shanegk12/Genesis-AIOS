Objectives, Constraints, and Variables

Concept Overview

Thinking Like an Engineer: The OCV Framework

Before building anything, engineers use a structured mindset. The OCV Framework helps us define our project's purpose (Objectives), what limits us (Constraints), and what we can change (Variables). We'll apply this to our mousetrap car to understand how to design and innovate effectively.

Designing Your Mousetrap Car: Objectives, Constraints, and Variables in Action

For our mousetrap car, our objective is to make it go the farthest. Our constraints include the laws of physics, the materials we have, and our time. The variables are the parts we can change, like wheel size or lever arm length, which we'll adjust and test to create the best car.

Optimizing Your Design: Science, Math, and Tradeoffs

To make our car better, we'll use science to understand how it works and math to measure our success. We'll learn about 'design tradeoffs,' where changing one variable might help in one way but hurt in another. Finding the right balance is key to optimizing our car's performance.

Harnessing Energy and Fighting Resistance

Our mousetrap car needs to transfer energy efficiently from the trap to the wheels. We'll also learn about 'resistive forces' like friction and drag, which can slow our car down. However, some friction is good, helping our wheels grip the ground so the car can move forward.

Key Takeaway

Engineering isn't just about building; it's about smart, intentional thinking. By using the OCV Framework, understanding tradeoffs, and managing energy and resistance, we can design and innovate our mousetrap cars to achieve amazing results through a cycle of continuous improvement.
