Prototypes

Concept Overview

Why Engineers Build to 'Fail'

In engineering, we don't expect our first ideas to be perfect. Instead, we build prototypes knowing they might not work perfectly right away. This helps us learn and discover new ways to improve our designs, pushing the boundaries of what's possible.

Your Prototyping Journey: From Mark 1.0 to 3.0

You'll build three physical versions of your mousetrap car: Mark 1.0 to get started, Mark 1.1 to experiment with changes, and Mark 2.0 to refine your best ideas. Finally, you'll create a Mark 3.0 computer model to test even more improvements digitally.

The Power of One Change: One Factor At A Time (OFAT)

To truly understand what makes your car better, you'll use the OFAT method. This means you'll only change one part of your car at a time, like the wheels or the lever arm, so you know exactly which change caused a difference in performance.

Key Takeaway

Prototyping is a journey of building, testing, and learning from each version of your design. By changing only one thing at a time, you become a detective, discovering exactly what makes your mousetrap car go further and faster!
