Power Transmission Mechanisms

Concept Overview

Spinning into Motion: Rotational Input

Imagine the mousetrap's arm snapping down – that's 'rotational input'! It's the circular motion that starts everything. This stored energy in the spring turns into a spin, which is the first step in making your car move.

From Spin to Zoom: Linear Travel

Once the axle spins, your wheels start turning, and that's how your car moves forward in a straight line – we call this 'linear travel'. The spinning motion of the wheels pushes the car across the floor. The bigger your wheels, the further your car will go with each spin!

Measuring the Journey: Calculating Distance

We can predict how far your car will travel! By knowing the size of your wheels (their circumference), we can figure out how much ground the car covers with each full rotation. We also consider how much the mousetrap's lever moves to understand how much the axle will turn.

Lost Energy: Why Cars Slow Down

Even the best designs lose some energy. Things like friction (surfaces rubbing together) or air resistance can slow your car down. Identifying these 'energy losses' helps you build a more efficient and faster mousetrap car!

Key Takeaway

Understanding how rotational motion from the mousetrap's lever turns into the linear motion of your car is crucial for designing a successful mousetrap car. Calculating distances and minimizing energy loss will help you make your car go further and faster!
