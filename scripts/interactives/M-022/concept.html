The Engineering Report Structure

Concept Overview

Why Your Report Matters (More Than the Car!)

You've built an awesome mousetrap car, but the real 'finish line' for an engineer is the Engineering Design Report (EDR). This report is like a map of your entire design journey, showing how you went from an idea to a working car. It proves your success wasn't luck, but smart, repeatable engineering.

Order from Chaos: The 'Map' of Your Work

Just like God brings order, your EDR brings order to your design process. It's how you 'write the vision' of your project, making it clear for others to understand your hard work and the truth behind your data. We'll learn the ten key parts of this professional report, using your mousetrap car as our example.

Integrity and Stewardship: Being a Truthful Engineer

Writing your EDR is an act of integrity – being honest, even about mistakes or challenges. It also shows your stewardship, how well you managed your time, talents, and energy. By finding order in your data, you're discovering the amazing faithfulness of God's physical laws.

From Student to Leader: Your Engineering Mandate

Your EDR shows you're not just a student, but a leader who can 'think, make, and optimize.' Aim for excellence, making your report clear enough for anyone to follow and backing up your choices with real data. Remember, a 'flaw' is just a 'finding' – keep iterating until your car performs its absolute best!

Key Takeaway

The Engineering Design Report (EDR) is your professional proof of concept, demonstrating your integrity, stewardship, and leadership by clearly documenting your design process, data, and decisions, turning your project into a repeatable scientific fact.
