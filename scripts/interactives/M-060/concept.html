Lesson: V2.0 Design in Sustainability

Concept Overview

Beyond 'Just Working': What is Sustainability?

Your mousetrap car worked, right? But great engineers don't stop there! This lesson is about making your designs even better by thinking about sustainability. Sustainability means meeting our needs today without messing up the future for others. For engineers, it means designing products that are kinder to our planet.

Designing for a Better Future: V2.0 Thinking

Imagine making a 'Version 2.0' of your mousetrap car. This time, you'll focus on making it more sustainable. We'll learn three cool strategies: 'Design for Disassembly' (making it easy to take apart), 'Material Substitution' (using better materials), and 'End-of-Life Planning' (thinking about what happens when it's no longer used). These ideas help us create less waste and use fewer new resources.

Strategy 1: Take It Apart! (Design for Disassembly)

How is your mousetrap car put together? If parts are glued, they're hard to recycle. 'Design for Disassembly' means building things so they can be easily taken apart. Think about using screws instead of glue, or making wheels that clip on. This way, different materials can be separated and recycled instead of ending up in a landfill.

Strategy 2: Smart Swaps! (Material Substitution)

Every material has an impact. For your V2.0 car, let's think about 'Material Substitution.' While balsa wood is a good choice, what about those plastic CD wheels? Could you use cardboard or plywood instead? Choosing materials that are easier to recycle or break down naturally helps reduce harm to the environment.

Key Takeaway

Designing for sustainability means thinking ahead, beyond just making something 'work.' By planning for disassembly, choosing better materials, and considering a product's end-of-life, you can create designs that are not only functional but also responsible and respectful of our planet's resources. You're becoming an engineer who builds a better future!
