Think of it like the nutrient cycle in nature — nothing truly goes to waste because every output becomes an input for something else. God built that kind of elegant efficiency into creation from the start. Engineers are learning to follow His lead.</p>"

      "html": "<h2>End-of-Life Options for Materials</h2><p>When an engineer thinks about a material, one of the most important questions is: <em>how does this material's story end?</em> There are several possible endings, and some are much better than others.</p><h3>Recycling</h3><p>Recycling breaks a material down and reforms it into something new. Aluminum is one of the best examples — it can be melted down and reformed almost indefinitely without losing quality. Paper, glass, and many plastics can also be recycled, though the process isn't always perfect. Recycling keeps materials out of landfills and reduces the need to extract new raw resources from the earth.</p><h3>Upcycling vs. Downcycling</h3><p>Not all recycling is equal. <strong>Upcycling</strong> takes a material and turns it into something of <em>higher</em> value — like turning old wooden pallets into furniture. <strong>Downcycling</strong> happens when recycling reduces a material's quality — like when high-grade plastic gets processed into lower-grade plastic that can only be used once more before it's truly waste. When you can, aim for upcycling. It's better stewardship.</p><h3>Reuse</h3><p>Sometimes the most sustainable choice is the simplest one — use it again. Reuse skips the energy-intensive recycling process entirely. If a component from your mousetrap car can be cleaned up and used in your next project, that's excellent engineering and excellent stewardship at the same time.</p><h3>Biodegradation</h3><p>Some materials, particularly natural ones like wood, cotton, or paper, are <strong>biodegradable</strong>. Given time and the right conditions, microorganisms break them down and return their elements to the soil. This is nature's own recycling system — the one God designed long before humans thought to imitate it.</p>"
